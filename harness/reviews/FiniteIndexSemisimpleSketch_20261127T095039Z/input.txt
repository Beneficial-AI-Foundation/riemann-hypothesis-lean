\title{Semisimplicity from a Finite-Index Subgroup}
\author{}

\maketitle

Let $\rho:\Gamma\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$ be a finite-dimensional representation
over a field $k$ of characteristic zero, and let $N\leq\Gamma$ have finite
index. Assume every $N$-invariant subspace has an $N$-invariant complement.
We show that every $\Gamma$-invariant subspace does too.

\section*{Main line}

Fix a $\Gamma$-invariant subspace $W$. Choose an $N$-invariant complement
$W'$ and let $p:V\to V$ be projection onto $W$ along $W'$. Gap 1 makes $p$
commute with $N$. Conjugate it by $\rho(g)$:
\[
  T_g=\rho(g)p\rho(g)^{-1}.
\]
Each $T_g$ maps into $W$ and fixes $W$. Gap 2 lets $T_g$ descend to the
finite set of left cosets $\Gamma/N$ and makes
$S=\sum_{q\in\Gamma/N}T_q$ commute with $\Gamma$. Since
$d=|\Gamma/N|$ is nonzero in $k$, $P=d^{-1}S$ is a
$\Gamma$-equivariant map into $W$ that fixes $W$. By Gap 3,
$\ker P$ is a $\Gamma$-invariant complement of $W$.

\section*{Gaps to prove independently}

\paragraph{Gap 1.}
If $V=W\oplus W'$ and both summands are $N$-invariant, prove that the
projection $p$ onto $W$ along $W'$ satisfies
$p\rho(n)=\rho(n)p$ for every $n\in N$.

\paragraph{Gap 2.}
Under Gap 1, prove $T_{gn}=T_g$ for $g\in\Gamma$, $n\in N$, and
$T_{hg}=\rho(h)T_g\rho(h)^{-1}$ for $h,g\in\Gamma$. Deduce that
$S=\sum_{q\in\Gamma/N}T_q$ is independent of coset representatives and
that $S\rho(h)=\rho(h)S$, by permuting the cosets with left multiplication.

\paragraph{Gap 3.}
If a linear map $P:V\to V$ has image in $W$, fixes every element of $W$,
and commutes with $\rho(\Gamma)$, prove $V=W\oplus\ker P$ and prove
$\ker P$ is $\Gamma$-invariant.

\section*{Allowed facts}

elementary properties of group representations, cosets, and linear algebra.
Do not assume semisimplicity for $\Gamma$.
